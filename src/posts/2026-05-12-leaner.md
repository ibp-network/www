Quick update on where the 2026 bounty top-up is at.

We put it on Subsquare as [post #443](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/posts/443) in February. It's been through a few rounds of feedback since, mostly from Parity and the Web3 Foundation pointing out places where we'd been generous to ourselves. The ask covers the 12 months from March 2026 to roughly March 2027 (anchored at the point the previous bounty ran out, not at proposal-pass date). Annual total: $1.42M, down from a first draft of $3.44M. Compensation is denominated in USD but paid in DOT, so the DOT amount actually drawn against the bounty floats with the exchange rate at each child-bounty release — at the current $1.41 7-day EMA that's roughly 1.02 MDOT, but the DOT figure moves with price. The $1.42M breaks down to $1.36M for relay-chain validators and system-parachain RPC, plus $58K for the surviving "global services" line — Fellowship GitHub runners, the GeoDNS layer, and curator/advisor compensation. Parachain RPC and the building fund are zero.

On top of that, $384K is retroactive for services rendered between March and mid-April that haven't been billed (the last bounty payout was February), and $18K is for Bulletin chain validators we've been running for Parity since September.

In DOT terms 1.02 MDOT is roughly flat to last year's [Ref 1516](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/referenda/1516), which was funded when DOT traded much higher and against which about $2.5M was actually drawn down over the year. The 58.68% reduction we lead with is what came off our own opening position after Parity and W3F's feedback, not what we cut off last year's run rate.

## Why this got smaller

The cut is real, and the underlying conditions are real.

Cost-cutting is the ecosystem-wide story right now, not an IBP thing. The 2024–2025 cycle that was meant to be a bull market didn't produce the growth that was priced into spending plans, and the broader crypto narrative has moved toward hard-money discipline. Polkadot's own halvening cycle reduces treasury inflation, so every treasury-funded programme is working against a smaller pool than it was a year ago.

The IBP-specific part of the cut is straightforward. Transaction volume isn't where it was in 2023. Most jurisdictions we operate in have stopped treating crypto businesses as inherently suspicious; the original case for being a seven-jurisdiction federation was deplatforming risk, and that risk is materially less acute than it was. Smoldot has shipped: for many dApp read paths, the right answer is a light client in the browser, with the IBP only providing the bootnodes that get smoldot onto the network. Polkadot's own planned 2026 app launch is built on light clients — the in-browser path is the default now, not a future feature. The IBP is meant to be a supportive layer (bootnodes, public RPC for the cold start, redundancy when chain teams need it), not a critical dependency the network can't function without.

What's still worth funding is the cost arbitrage. Compute is becoming the new real estate. Cloud pricing on the resources that matter for chain infrastructure (bandwidth, NVMe, dense CPUs) has roughly doubled in two years. Older hardware is appreciating rather than depreciating: LLMs are absorbing workloads that human labour used to carry, every flavour of compute is in demand, and boxes that would historically have aged out are holding their value or gaining it.

Concretely: the proposal's per-resource pricing lands roughly 15% below AWS / GCP 3-year reserved on vCPU and 23% below on NVMe — using their own published rates as the baseline, after their respective volume discounts. Against on-demand AWS or any managed-RPC offering (Infura / Alchemy / QuickNode), the gap is much larger. Treasury money that funds member-owned hardware supports a circular economy: operators paid in DOT, infrastructure spend kept inside the network's gravity rather than draining to big tech.

## What changed

External parachains exited the bounty rotation in March: Acala, Ajuna, Bifrost Polkadot, Nexus (Hyperbridge), Moonbeam, Unique. The `*.dotters.network` hostnames for those chains no longer route to IBP infrastructure. If your dApp was pointed at one, switch to the chain team's own RPC, or run smoldot against the chain spec.

Hydration is the exception. Same URLs, same operators, but funded directly by the Hydration treasury rather than the Polkadot one. We also run a quorum of Hydration collators as part of the arrangement. If anything else wants to use the IBP going forward, this is the shape.

System parachain coverage stays: Asset Hubs, Bridge Hubs, Coretime, People, Collectives, Encointer on Kusama. Full per-member redundancy is kept on the Polkadot and Kusama relay chains and on their Asset Hubs. Bridge Hubs, Coretime, People, Collectives, Encointer, and the entire Paseo side run on a reduced subset of operators.

Three design choices came up for review and we kept all of them. No API keys: the public-good case stops being credible the moment we require accounts. Per-member GeoDNS rather than a shared anycast prefix: anycast would hide which operator served which request, and we want that observable. Bootnodes in the official chain specs: this is what makes smoldot work without our infrastructure in the request path, and it's core to the mandate.

The rest of the savings came from cutting our own overhead. Curator compensation halved. Engineering is no longer a bounty line item at all.

## Talk to us

The cuts free up real capacity on our member hardware. If you build on Polkadot and you're costing out infrastructure of any kind — RPC, archive nodes, collators, indexer compute, bootnodes, validators — we'd rather have that workload running on member-owned boxes than on AWS.
