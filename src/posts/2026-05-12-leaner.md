The IBP has been treasury-funded since 2022, originally by Kusama (Ref 35) and from 2024 also by Polkadot. We've spent the last few months working through what the programme should look like going forward, and the result is a tighter, cheaper proposal that's now in the discussion phase on Subsquare. The new site is the visible part of that. The numbers below are the substantive part.

## The proposal, briefly

It's [post #443](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/posts/443) on Subsquare, still pre-referendum. The cycle funding the programme today is [Referendum 1516](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/referenda/1516), the 2025 top-up of the original 2024 bounty. Comments and amendments on #443 are still landing, and the version we've put forward is materially different from our first internal draft.

Forward spend as proposed: **$1.42M / year** (≈ 1.02 MDOT at the current 7d EMA of $1.41), a **58.68% reduction** vs. our **initial draft of this top-up cycle** after working through Parity and W3F's cost-saving feedback. Plus **$384K** retroactive for unpaid services and **$18K** for Bulletin validation. Full breakdown is in the [proposal text](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1vPPMRghpQYrFg_SjuQe31Nl1hcvTyuZ0z5OnyycqUTg/edit?tab=t.0).

(Cross-cycle USD comparisons get noisy because of DOT price moves. [Referendum 1516](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/referenda/1516) — last year's funded cycle — was a 1.03 MDOT bounty, funded when DOT was materially higher, of which ~$2.5M was actually drawn down operationally over the year. This year's ask is essentially flat to that in DOT terms, deliberately so; the 58.68% headline is "what we trimmed off our own opening position", not "what we cut off last year's run rate".)

If you want to push back before this goes to a vote, [Subsquare #443](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/posts/443) is the place.

## Parity's feedback, and what we did with it

Our first draft assumed continuing coverage of every parachain we'd ever onboarded. Parity's review, led by Erin Whitaker as Head of Infrastructure, came back direct: the treasury shouldn't subsidise RPC capacity that chains can fund themselves. Fair point, and we took it.

What that looked like in practice was a round of conversations with every external parachain we'd been serving. Same offer: we'll keep your coverage running, but it'll be a paid arrangement with your treasury instead of a line item in our bounty. **Hydration said yes.** The rest either had their own RPC story already or weren't in a position to take it on.

So under the new proposal the IBP stops serving Acala, Ajuna, Bifrost Polkadot, Nexus (Hyperbridge), Moonbeam, and Unique from the public bounty. If your dApp is pointed at a `*.dotters.network` host for one of those, switch to the chain team's own RPC, or run smoldot in-browser against the chain's spec file. Every Polkadot parachain ships a canonical chain spec; drop it into smoldot and you're trustlessly connected, no hosted RPC in the path.

Hydration deserves its own paragraph, because this is the part of the story that saves the DAO money. They were the largest external-parachain consumer of treasury-funded RPC. Under the new proposal **the Polkadot DAO stops paying for that capacity entirely.** The Hydration treasury picks it up directly, on terms negotiated chain-to-operator. The dApps keep working at the same URLs; the bill just moves. IBP members also run a quorum of Hydration collators as part of the deal, so operators get an incentive aligned with block production while the chain gets distributed collator capacity. The public RPC stays public; the public treasury stops funding it. That's the template we want for any future external-chain coverage.

What's left on the bounty is system infrastructure: relay chains (validators only, see below), Asset Hubs on Polkadot / Kusama / Paseo, Bridge Hubs, Coretime, People, Collectives, and Encointer on Kusama.

## What this means if you build on Polkadot

Asset Hub Polkadot doesn't move. `wss://asset-hub-polkadot.dotters.network`, GeoDNS-routed, no keys, no limits, sub-50ms to most of the world. Same for Asset Hub Kusama. Mainnet workloads keep full per-member redundancy.

Paseo is where the redundancy cut shows up. It's a testnet, so it doesn't need 24/7 multi-region failover, and under this proposal a smaller subset of operators serves it. Same hostname, fewer boxes behind it. Day-to-day testing won't notice; don't put a CI critical path on it.

## Three things we wouldn't budge on

The review questioned three pieces of the design. We pushed back, kept all three, and the proposal carries the same answers we've given since 2024.

The first was public, unauthenticated RPC. Reviewers floated API-key gating. We refused. The moment you require accounts you've reinvented Infura, and the whole point of the IBP is to be the thing Polkadot has that Ethereum doesn't. The new cost structure is sustainable without keys, so there's no honest reason to add them.

The second was per-member GeoDNS routing. A simpler shared anycast cluster was suggested. Anycast and well-tuned GeoDNS land in roughly the same latency envelope; which one wins depends on configuration, not on the technology, so latency wasn't actually the deciding factor either way. We kept GeoDNS-per-member because each operator owns their own AS and IPs, and those are verifiable against the canonical config. The routing layer makes the programme's operator transparency observable. A shared anycast prefix would have hidden it.

The third was bootnodes in the official chain specs. This is what makes smoldot in-browser work without our infra in the path, and it's core to the mandate, not a discretionary nice-to-have. So it stayed.

## We cut our own overhead too

The 58.68% headline reduction isn't only about dropping parachains. The proposal also halves what the programme pays for coordination.

Curator fees come down by half. The original 2024 bounty allocated a meaningful slice to curators, the bounty-contract role that releases child-bounty payouts. That overhead is roughly fixed regardless of programme size, and the programme just got smaller, so the same shape applies to curator compensation.

Development hours come down materially as well. A lot of the programme's historical engineering spend went into tooling that wrapped Polkadot's APIs: dashboards, ingestion pipelines, monitoring glue. AI coding assistants plus the now-mature PAPI / reactive-dot / Dedot client ecosystem have lowered the bar enough that this work no longer needs a dedicated engineer-month per quarter. One operator can now ship in a week what would have taken a few weeks a year ago. The budget reflects that, instead of holding the old line out of habit.

The bounty should fund the work that still needs paid hours. The work that tooling now absorbs shouldn't be on it.

## Spare capacity, on the other side

There's a flip side to cutting bounty-funded coverage on chains that don't need it. Our members now have real spare capacity, and we'd like to use it. Same shape as the Hydration deal: chain treasury (or wallet, or indexer, or whoever) pays, IBP members run the workload, GeoDNS routing and operator transparency come along for the ride.

If you're a parachain team, an indexer, a wallet, or anyone with infra that benefits from globally distributed, member-owned, operator-aligned hosting, come talk to us. RPC, collators, archive nodes, bootnodes, validation pools, or something we haven't done before.

We're not limiting that offer to Polkadot. If you're running a different chain (L1, L2, app-chain, doesn't matter) and you want the same kind of independent, geographically distributed validator-grade hosting, the conversation is open. Our bias is towards networks that take operator decentralisation seriously and play well with the broader web3 stack; if that's you, the door's open even if your chain doesn't share a runtime with ours. The point of the leaner programme is that we now have the room.
